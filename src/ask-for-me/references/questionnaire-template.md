# <Questionnaire title>

**Purpose:** why this questionnaire exists and the decision riding on it.

**From:** <the user>. **To:** <the recipient>. **How your answers will be used:** <where they go>.

## Context

One paragraph orienting a recipient who wasn't in the user's head. Enough to answer well, not a page.

## How to answer

Deadline and rough effort, and what happens if we hear nothing by then: "If we have no answer by <date>, we go ahead with <the default>." Partial answers and "I don't know" are useful. Flag anything you are unsure of rather than skipping it.

## <Theme heading>

One `##` section per theme. Under each, its questions, most important first, because async means you may only get one pass. Every question is one idea, never compound, with an answer stub directly beneath, and a one-line _why this matters_ only where the question could be misread or invite a throwaway answer. Ask for recognition, never recall: where the repo, the tracker, or the user's own materials give a partial answer, the question states that reading for the recipient to confirm or correct, because a bare "why is this 300 seconds?" is an exam a busy person answers with a plausible guess — their time goes only on what is written nowhere. Within that, the reading offered for confirmation is itself specific — a number, a date, a named failure, a named alternative — never a summary of the area: recognition decides the question's form, this decides what it asks for. Where the question exists because two people disagree, it states both positions and names the evidence that would settle it — a measurement, a document, a person — for the recipient to supply or point to, never the two positions alone.
