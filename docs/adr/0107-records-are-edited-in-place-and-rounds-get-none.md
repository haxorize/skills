# Decision records are edited in place, and rounds get none

This supersedes [ADR-0037](0037-adr-gate-discriminates-amendment-form.md) (the gate picks an additive amendment form) and [ADR-0074](0074-corrected-consequence-marker-and-check.md) (the corrected-consequence marker and its check).

A record states the decision as it stands today. A change that leaves a record's title decision standing is made by editing the record; git history is the log of what changed and the commit message says why. A change that undoes the title gets a new record that supersedes the old one, as before. No new `## Amendments` entries, no `This amends` records with `> **Amended by**` pointers, and no `— settled:`, `— corrected:` or `— amended: see Amendments <date>` markers. A record that still carries an older log has it folded into its body the next time it is edited for any reason, except a superseded record, whose Status line is the only edit it takes and which is otherwise left as written; nothing is rewritten in bulk. The rule ships to every repo through `adr`'s format reference (`src/adr/references/adr-format.md` § Edit or write new).

A mining round, a batch, or a batch family is a log of work done, not a decision, and gets no record of its own. A borrowed idea's credit lives in `docs/lineage.md`, and a round closes with the commit that deletes `CLAUDE.md`'s `## Round` block. The 20 records whose whole content is a round's or batch's log stay where they are, unedited, as history; a record that names a round or batch but carries a decision that outlives it (ADR-0050, ADR-0062, ADR-0089 to ADR-0093) is a live record and is edited like any other. The 20: [ADR-0046](0046-four-repo-fragment-mine.md), [ADR-0047](0047-open-code-review-fragment-mine.md), [ADR-0051](0051-usage-evidence-mining-round.md), [ADR-0073](0073-team-fit-mining-round.md), [ADR-0075](0075-transcript-sourced-mining-round.md), [ADR-0077](0077-tightening-round-one-house-style.md), [ADR-0081](0081-batch-2-scanner-admissions-2026-09-04.md), [ADR-0082](0082-batch-family-4a-4f-admissions-2026-09-04.md), [ADR-0083](0083-batch-family-4g-4k-admissions-2026-09-04.md), [ADR-0084](0084-batch-family-6-7-admissions-2026-09-04.md), [ADR-0086](0086-batch-3-admissions-2026-09-12.md), [ADR-0087](0087-batch-4-admissions-2026-09-12.md), [ADR-0088](0088-batch-6-admissions-2026-09-12.md), [ADR-0094](0094-close-of-the-2026-09-12-mining-round.md), [ADR-0095](0095-batch-3-admissions-2026-09-26.md), [ADR-0096](0096-batch-4-admissions-2026-09-26.md), [ADR-0097](0097-batch-5-admissions-2026-09-26.md), [ADR-0098](0098-batch-6-admissions-2026-09-26.md), [ADR-0099](0099-batch-7-admissions-2026-09-26.md), [ADR-0100](0100-close-of-the-2026-09-26-mining-round.md). Some rules still in force have their only written reason inside one of them (how a report stamps the tree it measured, why the security scanner flags a skill file that is merely named). When such a rule changes, its reason goes beside the rule, in the script or rule file and the commit, and a new record is written only if the change clears the gate on its own.

The measurement behind the change, from the 2026-10-12 round (`~/code/lib/_rounds/2026-10-12/themes/process.md`): amendment logs were 557,699 of the corpus's 1,253,578 bytes (44%), the 20 round and batch records 581,699 bytes (46%), and one record alone 187,656 bytes. Each correction cost three edits (the entry, the marker, the pointer) where one would do, and writing round and batch records took about five sessions in a month. Nick chose this on 2026-10-12 (grill Q2, and Q34 for folding on next edit rather than in bulk).

**The gate.** Hard to reverse: every record written from here follows the new form, and other repos take it from the `adr` skill, where a11y-health-api already uses amendment logs in 21 of its 50 records. Surprising without context: a reader finds 20 records with long dated logs, newer ones with none, and a lint script that no longer reads the logs. A real trade-off: the additive log keeps a record's earlier wording readable in the file itself, without `git log`, which is what ADR-0037 chose and a team can reasonably prefer.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the additive log and drop only the markers.** Rejected: the log is where the bytes are, and a reader still has to merge body and log in their head to learn what holds today.
- **Move the 20 round records to a `docs/rounds/` folder.** Rejected: the move rewrites 119 links, and no skill body cites them.
- **Rewrite every record's log into its body now.** Rejected (grill Q34): 1.25 MB of edits nobody asked for, against folding each record when someone next has a reason to edit it.

## Consequences

- `scripts/lint-adrs.sh` drops its five amendment checks (the forward pointer, and the settled, corrected, amended and in-log markers). Numbering, supersession and its back-link, both `Revisit when:` forms, and cross-references stay. [ADR-0068](0068-lint-adrs-enforces-two-way-pointers.md) is edited to match.
- `CLAUDE.md` § Commit order drops its admissions-record clause (58 B every turn in this repo).
- [ADR-0079](0079-per-turn-load-ceilings-are-permanent.md)'s per-batch spend log ends. Its ceilings stay, since lint enforces them, and a spend nobody logged is no longer a review finding.
- A teammate reading an edited record sees only the current wording; the earlier one is in `git log -p` on that file.

Revisit when: a reader, in this repo or one that takes the `adr` skill, needs a record's earlier wording often enough that `git log -p` is the usual way they read a record.
