# `docs/adr/` gets its own linter, and the two-way forward pointer is enforced rather than relaxed

`adr-format.md` has required, since [ADR-0016](0016-behavior-skills-declared-deps-relaxed-atomicity.md)'s day, that a record amending another by a new number be linked both ways: the amender says `This amends [ADR N](file)` and the amended record carries `> **Amended by [ADR N](file):** …` at its top. Nothing checked it, and it drifted: the 2026-08-27 round measured the link form at 3 missing pointers in 7 pairs; the first run of the check below over the link and bold forms found 7 missing in 12 at `de9ae10` — `0036` and `0045` (amended by 0052), `0041` (by 0057), `0051` (by 0058), and the three older bold-form pairs `0007` (by 0016), `0010` (by 0017) and `0016` (by 0020), two of which say in their own text that the target carries, or is to carry, an amendment note pointing here (0017 as a statement that was false when written, 0016 as a promise never kept); and the 2026-08-29 review's widened read — every link on a line, the unlinked prose form, the `[ADR N]` space form the format reference prescribes — found 9 missing in 17 at the same tree, the two more being `0014` (by 0044) and `0043` (by 0057), both of whose amenders say the pointer exists. A reader opening the amended record saw a decision that no longer stood as written, with no sign of it. The two ways to close that were to enforce the pointer or to change the format so the amended record's `## Amendments` log is the pointer; the grill chose enforcement, and this record is where the choice is visible.

`scripts/lint-adrs.sh` now runs over `docs/adr/` and fails on: a number claimed by two files; a `superseded by [ADR-N](file)` link whose target is missing or does not link back; an amend mention (the link, bold, or unlinked prose form, read outside the record's own `## Amendments` log) whose target lacks the forward pointer above its first `## ` heading; a `Revisit when:` line with nothing after the colon, in either the inline or the heading form; and a `## Deferred` line marked `— settled: see Amendments <date>` with no entry opening on that date. It exits 2, never 0, over a directory with nothing to check. `scripts/lint-adrs-selftest.sh` grades every check in both directions against `scripts/lint-fixtures/adr/` and `scripts/lint-fixtures-clean/adr/`, including the quiet forms — a pointer above the heading, a third-party "X amends Y" inside a log, the `, later` and `date — title.` entry openers — and the four exit codes. The seven pointers were written in the same change and the two more in its fix pass; nothing else in the records moved, and each pointer summarizes what its amender's own sentence says moved. `0036` gained a third pointer for `0045`'s in-place entry of 2026-08-08, which claims one — an in-place amendment owes no pointer under the format, but a record that says a pointer exists gets one.

**The gate.** Surprising without context: a reader finds a fourth lint script and a pointer line at the top of nine records with no sign of why either is there. A real trade-off: relax the format so the amended record's log is the pointer, against enforce the pointer as written — a rational team picks the log form, and [ADR-0037](0037-adr-gate-discriminates-amendment-form.md) nearly did. Hard to reverse in the corpus rather than the code: nine pointers written under one rule are the precedent every next amender copies.

## Considered Options

- **Accept the `## Amendments` log as the pointer.** Rejected: an in-place amendment appends to the log of the record it changes, but a new-number amendment appends to the *amender's* record, so the amended one's log stays empty and the reader still opens it cold. The log form answers the wrong file.
- **Fix the seven by hand and leave the format unchecked.** Rejected on the same evidence that motivated the check: the rule was written, cited, and still drifted at 9 in 17, and the next drift lands the same way.
- **Fold the checks into `scripts/lint-skills.sh`.** Rejected: that script's scope is the shipped skill tree and it walks `src/` and `global/rules/`; `docs/adr/` never hoists, its consumers are `adr`, `backfill-adrs` and the review, and a separate script keeps each header an honest account of what its own run reaches.

## Consequences

- The lint family — every script under `scripts/` — shares one exit-code taxonomy from here: 0 clean, 1 at least one FAIL, 2 nothing (or not everything) checked, 3 usage error (`DOMAIN.md` carries the row). `lint-skills.sh` adopts it in the same change — `--help` exits 0 and runs nothing, an unknown argument exits 3, a `LINT_ROOT` that is not a directory exits 2 rather than linting whatever directory the shell was in — and `lint-skills-selftest.sh` already used 2 for its partial outcome. The fix pass brought the two members that did not fit: `security.sh` exits 2 for a target that is not a directory and 3 for a usage error, and `skill-usage.sh` exits 2 on a run whose count carries a `+` floor. `scripts/git-hooks/post-merge` is the one automated reader of the number; its own selftest proves it reaches all three branches, which under the `set -e` it ran with before the fix pass it did not.
- The linter is gated: `post-merge` and `sweep-corpus` derive their rosters from the `<script>-selftest.sh` pairing, `lint-skills.sh` fails a `scripts/*.sh` with no selftest, and `scripts/selftest-lib.sh` is the one home for the matcher and the three-state close every selftest uses. The grill chose enforcement over a hand-fixed corpus because a rule nobody runs drifts; a linter nobody runs would have drifted the same way.
- Two `Revisit when:` forms are both accepted, the inline line `adr-format.md` prescribes and the `## Revisit when:` heading two 2026-08-2x records use; the format reference is not narrowed to one. Likewise two settled-entry openers: `- **<date>** —` and `- **<date> — title.**`, which `0060` uses throughout.
- `scripts/lint-selftest.sh` is renamed `scripts/lint-skills-selftest.sh` in the same change, so every selftest in the repo is `<script>-selftest.sh` — the shape the selftest-pairing checks in `lint-skills.sh` now enforce for `global/hooks/` and for `scripts/`. Records older than this one name the old file; they are history and are not rewritten.
- The bold `**Amends ADR-N:**` form is reached and kept legal; the three records that use it predate the link form and rewriting them would break "amendments never rewrite the original".
- Scope stated so a pass is not read as more than it is: an amend mention is read on one line, in the link, bold, and unlinked prose forms, where `amends` sits directly before the `[ADR`, `ADR-N`, or `**` token; the check judges the pointer's presence and placement, never whether its summary is true; a record amended in place, under its own log, needs and gets no pointer.
- Two judgment calls made in the same batch, recorded here because nothing else in the tree says why: `lint-skills.sh`'s 15,000-byte re-attach bound is a WARN and not a FAIL because eight shipped bodies already exceed a bound the platform owns, and a FAIL would red the tree on a figure a release can move; and `security.sh`'s remote-instructions rule drops `latest` as a per-run cue on purpose — it false-positived on "fetch the latest release notes before a major upgrade" — with the object of the fetch (guidelines, rules, instructions) as the narrower signal the 2026-08-29 review proposed and this record does not adopt. — corrected: see Amendments 2026-09-01

Revisit when: a consuming repo's ADR convention is a single-file decision log (`adr-format.md` names the variant), which none of these checks can read — the linter is this repo's, and it says so.

## Amendments

- **2026-09-01 — the re-attach bound is a FAIL, and the reason this record gave for the WARN is spent.** The bullet above grounded the WARN on eight shipped bodies already over a bound the platform owns, and on a FAIL reddening the tree over a figure a release can move. The first half no longer holds: [ADR-0077](0077-tightening-round-one-house-style.md)'s tightening round brought every body under 15,000 bytes, and its 2026-09-01 Batch M-size entry records the flip of `check_reattach_bytes` to a FAIL, extended to `references/*.md` as `check_reference_bytes`, on the premise the round created — that no file is over. The second half stands as a cost that was accepted rather than answered: a release that moves the token-to-byte ratio still reds the `pre-commit` hook, and the round's answer is that a body which re-crosses takes a relocation. `security.sh`'s `latest` judgment in the same bullet is untouched by this entry. Nothing in this record's own decision — the two-way pointer and the linter that grades it — changes here; the bullet is marked because a reader who stops at Consequences would otherwise act on a WARN the tree no longer has.
