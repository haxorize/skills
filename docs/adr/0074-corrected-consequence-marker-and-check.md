# A Consequences bullet a later amendment corrects is marked in place, and the marker is graded

Amendments in this corpus are additive. A dated entry is appended to `## Amendments` and the record's original text is never rewritten, so a reader can trust the body to say what was decided at the time rather than what the author later wished had been decided. That stays. Its cost is that a `## Consequences` bullet whose figure a later amendment corrects still reads as current, and Consequences is a section readers stop at. [ADR-0072](0072-shared-trigger-phrase-check-and-never-ran-is-not-clean.md)'s bullet said nine control-clean rules had been retained on one ground; its 2026-08-29 amendment re-counted them at seven on two grounds, and the bullet stood unmarked, leaving the corpus with one place where the nine still read as the current figure. `## Deferred` already had an affordance for exactly this shape — a settled line keeps its text and gains a trailing `— settled: see Amendments <date>` — and Consequences had none. It now takes the same form, `— corrected: see Amendments <date>`, prescribed in `adr-format.md` beside the settled marker, and `scripts/lint-adrs.sh`'s `check_corrected_consequence` fails a marker whose date opens no amendment.

The decision is the user's, taken on an escalation out of the 2026-08-29 batch-12 review of the round [ADR-0073](0073-team-fit-mining-round.md) records, which raised the gap as finding F75 and costed the two branches without picking one. The alternative — accept that Consequences can go stale and say so in a scope sentence — is the cheaper change and a position a reasonable team holds, since Consequences records what a decision was expected to cause and a reader wanting the current picture can read the amendment log. It lost on where the reader stands: the log is the file's tail, the stale bullet is in its middle, and nothing in the middle tells the reader the tail exists.

**The gate.** Hard to reverse: the marker is a corpus-wide convention living in three byte-identical `adr-format.md` siblings plus a graded check, and every correcting amendment from here copies it — withdrawing it means unmarking every marked bullet and dropping the check that grades them. Surprising without context: a reader finds a bullet the record itself calls superseded, left standing, and wonders why the author did not simply fix the number. A real trade-off: the two branches above, one of which was argued for in the review that raised it.

## Considered Options

- **Accept the staleness and scope it in `adr-format.md`.** Rejected, and it is the branch the review costed as cheaper. It documents the failure rather than closing it, and it documents it in the reference an author reads while writing rather than in the record a reader reads afterwards.
- **Extend the marker but leave it ungraded.** Rejected on [ADR-0068](0068-lint-adrs-enforces-two-way-pointers.md)'s own evidence: the two-way pointer rule was written, cited, and still drifted to 9 missing pointers in 17 pairs. A convention nothing runs against drifts, and this one has fewer occasions to be noticed than that one did.
- **Rewrite the corrected bullet in place and note the change in the amendment.** Rejected: it breaks the additive rule across the whole corpus to fix one section, and the record's body stops being evidence of what was decided.

## Consequences

- Two bullets carry the marker — `grep -rn "— corrected: see Amendments" docs/adr/` returns `0072:24` and `0007:25`, beside this record's own mentions of the marker's text. A correcting amendment now owes a third, and the two that exist are the precedent it copies.
- The check grades the pointer's presence and its date, never whether the correction is right or whether an uncorrected bullet should have been marked. A record whose Consequences went stale and whose author noticed nothing still passes.
- The marker's scope is `## Consequences`, deliberately: body and `## Deferred` prose a later amendment overtakes stays unmarked and is corrected by the amendment log alone — [ADR-0065](0065-product-description-model-invoked-not-onboard-me-phase-0.md)'s improvised "(superseded — see Amendments)" notes are the ancestor this scope declines to standardise. The accepted cost is a reader caught mid-body by a sentence the log has overtaken.
- `check_xref_target` landed in the same commit and takes no record of its own. Grading that a `](NNNN-*.md)` link resolves to a file enforces a rule nobody disputes, and ADR-0068 owns the linter's existence and scope. It is named here so a reader who finds the two checks landing together, and a record behind only `check_corrected_consequence`, knows the second omission was deliberate.
- The convention lives in three byte-identical `adr-format.md` copies under `src/adr/`, `src/grill-me/` and `src/backfill-adrs/`, registered in `sibling_groups`. Changing the wording moves all three or fails `check_sibling_identity`.

Revisit when: an amendment corrects only part of a Consequences bullet, so one trailing marker cannot say which half is superseded — the case the single-marker form was not designed to carry.
